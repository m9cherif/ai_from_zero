This is a base model, not an assistant. It was trained to continue text, so it has no notion of questions, instructions or facts. It invents names, drifts between prose and verse — Dickens, Austen, Melville, Dostoevsky, Shakespeare and Milton are all in its training data — and forgets what it said 256 tokens ago. At 8.3M parameters it is roughly a thousandth the size of a modern chat model.

The larger model learned which words are common and stopped there. Filling a billion parameters takes roughly 18 billion tokens; this corpus has 25 million, so it saw 0.002% of what it needed. Its loss sat at 6.93 across eight measurements spanning a sevenfold range of training — a flat line, not slow progress. Capacity you cannot afford to fill is worse than capacity you do not have, because every step costs 200× more to take.

It is absent from this page for a plainer reason too: int8 weights would make the download about 1.2 GB against a 16 MB budget, need roughly 3.7 GB of browser memory, and generate about one token every three seconds.
